How "Coolie" Became Associated with Indentured Laborers:
Indentured laborers from India, China, and later other countries (like Japan and Portuguese territories) were recruited for long-term labor contracts in the colonies of the British, French, and Dutch empires, particularly in the Caribbean, Fiji, Mauritius, and South Africa.

The use of the term "coolie" in this context can be broken down into two parts:

Pre-Indenture "Coolie": This refers to a day laborer or someone who worked on a short-term, often informal basis, without the structure of an official contract.

In India, this could include anyone hired temporarily for low-wage, menial work (often in rural areas).

Indentured "Coolie": Over time, the word "coolie" became strongly associated with those who signed long-term indentured contracts to work on plantations, in mines, or on public works in various colonies.

These were indentured laborers who worked under strict conditions, often for 5–10 years, to pay off their passage to the colony.

Differences in Usage:
"Indentured laborers" refers to people who signed formal, legally binding contracts that stipulated the terms of their work, including wages, living conditions, and the duration of service (usually 5 years, with the possibility of renewal).
