Religious practices, such as Hinduism and Islam, were central to the indentured laborers’ identities, and their continued practice in Suriname is also an important aspect of cultural reparations.

Many members of the community call for support for religious institutions and practices that are rooted in the indentured experience, such as the establishment of temples, mosques, and cultural centers dedicated to preserving these traditions.

Intergenerational Legacy and Healing
Some community members see reparations as a means of healing, not just for themselves but for future generations.

By recognizing the injustice of the indenture system, they hope to break the cycle of trauma and build a better future for their children and grandchildren.

Intergenerational Trauma
Shalini, a mental health counselor, speaks about the intergenerational trauma experienced by Indo-Surinamese families.

She explains that many individuals in the community carry the psychological scars of their ancestors’ abuse, even if they did not personally experience indenture.

"The stories of struggle and suffering were passed down to us in our families.
