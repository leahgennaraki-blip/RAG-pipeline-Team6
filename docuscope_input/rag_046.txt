Indentured laborers on plantations engaged in various forms of protest, responding to the harsh conditions and oppressive systems they faced.

The protests, while varied, often did not challenge state power outright or aim to overthrow authority but rather sought to diminish the worst effects of the indenture system and express dissent within the limited power the laborers held.

Here are the key forms of protest observed:

1.

**Everyday Resistance**: This included subtle, non-confrontational acts like malingering, feigning illness, slowing work pace, and sabotage such as breaking tools and damaging crops.

Such actions were intended to undermine plantation productivity without sparking direct conflict with authority figures.

2.

**Absenteeism and Desertion**: Indentured laborers often absented themselves from work or deserted the plantations entirely.
