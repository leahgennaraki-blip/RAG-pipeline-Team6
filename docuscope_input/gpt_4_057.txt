The indentured system in Suriname officially came to an end in 1916, though the process of dismantling the system began earlier, and its aftermath had significant consequences for the Indian population in Suriname.

The legacy of the indentured labor system shaped the socio-economic, cultural, and political landscape for Indo-Surinamese communities in profound ways.

Gradual Phasing Out of Indenture
Indenture as a system of labor was employed by the Dutch colonial authorities to fill the labor shortages left by the abolition of slavery.

Initially, the system was set up to provide labor for the sugar plantations in Suriname.

The Indian indentured laborers, often referred to as "coolies", were brought in primarily from India between 1873 and 1916.

The Dutch government and plantation owners began to reduce the intake of indentured laborers towards the early 20th century.
