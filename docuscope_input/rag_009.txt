The income of indentured labourers during the period of indenture varied, but the contracts often included specific terms for their wages.

In many British colonies, such as Fiji and Trinidad, the wage agreements mandated that male labourers earn one shilling per day, while female labourers were to receive nine pennies per day.

However, these terms often did not reflect the reality of their earnings due to several factors.

1.

**Wages on Paper vs.

Actual Earnings:** The contracted wages were often the maximum amount that could be earned under ideal conditions.

In practice, various deductions and the failure to fulfill tasks due to overwork or illness often meant that labourers were unable to reach these maximum earnings.

For instance, in Fiji, records show it was not until 1908, nearly three decades after the onset of indenture, that adult male labourers could average a wage of one shilling per day.
