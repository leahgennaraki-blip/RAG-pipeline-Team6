**Colonial and Post-Colonial Treatment**:
- The colonial regimes used legal and administrative frameworks to ensure an endless supply of cheap labor, depicting "coolies" as inherently absconding, untrustworthy, or lazy, necessitating strict control through punitive regulations and sometimes violent enforcement.

- Over time, as the oppressive nature of these systems became clear, reforms started to take place, spurred by nationalist movements from the countries of origin, labor activism, and international pressure.

However, the scars of these systems lingered in the form of racial stereotypes, social marginalization, and economic disenfranchisement.

In summary, while all indentured laborers could be (and often were) called "coolies," not all references to "coolies" denote indentured laborers in a strictly contractual sense.

The conflation arises from cultural and historical narratives that treated these diverse laborers as a monolithic group beneath their white employers, driven by global colonialist agendas.
