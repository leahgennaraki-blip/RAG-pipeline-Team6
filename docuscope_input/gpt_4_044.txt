These organizations often took up the cause of better working conditions, equal rights, and justice for the Indian labor community.

Figures like Sookdeo Bissoondoyal in Mauritius and Pandit Raghunandan in Trinidad advocated for better conditions and political rights for indentured laborers, both during and after the indenture system.

8.

Resistance Through Family and Gender Roles
Women played a significant role in the forms of resistance on the plantations.

In addition to their traditional domestic roles, many Indian women found ways to resist by participating in small-scale protests or challenging the patriarchal structures imposed by the colonial system.

Women’s resistance often centered on issues such as family separation, the abduction of women, and the exploitation of women workers by plantation overseers.

Family structures in the indentured system also helped create spaces of resistance.

The family unit offered protection and solidarity, with parents working to provide better futures for their children despite the constraints imposed by colonial labor systems.

9.

Use of Languages and Codes
Language, particularly Bhojpuri, played an important role in resistance.

The language acted as a form of collective identity and also enabled laborers to communicate secretly or use codes to evade detection by overseers.
