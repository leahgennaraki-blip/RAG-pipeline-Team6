**Cultural Transmission Through Folk Songs:**
The indentured laborers brought with them a rich tradition of folk songs, especially in the bidesia style, which conveyed their emotions of sorrow, happiness, and nostalgia.

These songs were a crucial way the migrants maintained a connection to their homeland while adapting to their new environment.

The Bhojpuri folk songs embedded with themes of separation and longing were transformed to reflect the new realities and challenges faced in Suriname.

These songs often served both as a means of emotional expression and resistance to their conditions, enabling them to sustain their cultural identity amid adversity.

### 2.

**Development of Sarnami:**
In Suriname, as part of the broader phenomenon of diaspora language evolution, Bhojpuri combined with other Indian languages and dialects to develop into a unique linguistic form known as Sarnami.

Sarnami became the lingua franca for communication among the Indian community in Suriname, incorporating aspects of Bhojpuri, Awadhi, and other dialects from northern India, as well as influences from the local languages, especially African languages.

This linguistic amalgamation reflects how the Bhojpuri-speaking community negotiated identity and unity in a multicultural society.
