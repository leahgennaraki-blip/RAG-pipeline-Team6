**Creolization and Blended Social Practices**: Aside from linguistic creolization, other aspects of culture, such as agricultural practices, culinary traditions, and arts, were creolized, drawing from Dutch, Indian, and indigenous African influences.

Initiatives like cricket matches or gamelans (Indonesian musical ensembles) promoted by plantations and colonial administrations contributed to cultural exchanges and shared experiences.

### Government Policies and Colonial Structures

1.

**Government Assimilation Policies**: Initially, the Dutch colonial government encouraged assimilation into Dutch culture, promoting Dutch language, law, and customs.

However, the lack of a coherent integration policy meant practices were often pragmatic and ad hoc, adjusted as circumstances and colonial needs changed.

Education policies saw shifts from imposition of Dutch-centric systems to more inclusive approaches that allowed scope for cultural continuities.

2.

**Settlement Schemes**: The government implemented policies that encouraged permanent settlement through land allocations, intending to transition immigrants from plantation workers to smallholders.

While this did not immediately lead to widespread assimilation, it marked a shift from temporary labor policy to fostering a more permanent, though pluralistic, society.

3.

**Political and Legislative Integration**: Despite cultural contributions, political integration proved more challenging for the Indian community.
