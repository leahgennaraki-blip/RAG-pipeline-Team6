Many of the Indian indentured laborers started their lives in Suriname with very little, but over time, their descendants have ascended the social ladder.

Today, people of Indian descent in Suriname are involved in various professions, including business, politics, and education.

Land ownership became a central aspect of upward mobility for many indentured laborers.

After completing their indentureship, many sought to acquire small plots of land, thus becoming small farmers and gradually moving out of the poverty that characterized their early years in Suriname.

Over generations, this allowed them to build more stable economic lives.

However, there were also challenges tied to the continuation of poverty, especially for those who remained in more manual labor or agricultural work.

Despite these challenges, many in the community acknowledge the deep-rooted resilience that helped shape their economic and social progress.

Caste and Social Identity
The caste system, as it had existed in India, also left an imprint on the social structure of the Indian community in Suriname.

While caste was not formally enforced by the Dutch colonial system, it was still evident in aspects of social interaction, marriage, and community organization.
