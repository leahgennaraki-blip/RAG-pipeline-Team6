Dutch Involvement in Suriname’s Colonial History
The Netherlands was heavily involved in both the slave trade and the indenture system.

From the mid-19th century, following the abolition of slavery in Suriname (1863), the Dutch sought to replace enslaved labor with indentured workers from India, with many arriving under exploitative and violent conditions.

Indentured workers faced horrific living and working conditions, which included harsh physical labor, poor health care, inadequate food, and inadequate pay.

Many also experienced physical violence from plantation owners and overseers.

This violence was systemic, with the Dutch colonial authorities allowing plantation owners considerable autonomy over the labor force, which resulted in severe mistreatment of the indentured workers.

Despite the brutality of the system, the Dutch government did not formally recognize the full extent of the exploitation and violence until much later.
