"Coolie" was a more generalized and often pejorative term applied to unskilled laborers, particularly in the context of colonial exploitation.

The term became racialized and was used to denigrate these workers, highlighting the dehumanizing aspect of colonial labor systems.

The Colonial Context:
During the colonial era, the term "coolie" was used to refer to laborers from various parts of Asia (mainly India and China), regardless of their formal status.

However, once these laborers were recruited through indenture systems (as was the case for the vast majority of Indian laborers), they were technically indentured laborers — but "coolie" continued to be the more widely used, derogatory term in many places.

The term "coolie" often carried with it negative connotations of racial inferiority and inferiority of labor, emphasizing the colonial perspective that these workers were disposable and their labor was cheap.

Modern Usage:
Today, the term "coolie" is considered offensive, particularly in places where Indo-Caribbean, Indo-Fijian, and other Asian diaspora communities are prominent.

The word is seen as a legacy of colonialism and is associated with the exploitation and racism faced by laborers in the past.
