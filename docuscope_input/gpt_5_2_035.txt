Inter-racial relationships did occur on plantations, but they were unequal, highly regulated, and socially contentious.

Their reception varied sharply depending on who was involved, the power imbalance, and colonial racial hierarchies.

In most cases, such relationships were tolerated by colonial authorities when they served control, and stigmatized or punished within communities, especially when women bore the consequences.
