Conclusion
The resistance of indentured laborers in Suriname and other colonies was multi-faceted, ranging from quiet defiance in the form of work slowdowns and faked illnesses, to open protests such as strikes, uprisings, and desertion.

These laborers resisted in ways that were shaped by their cultural and religious practices, as well as the harsh realities they faced under the colonial plantation system.

Despite the oppressive conditions, they managed to maintain their human dignity and cultural identity, paving the way for future struggles for freedom and justice.
