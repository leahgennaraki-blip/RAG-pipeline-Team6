Many indentured laborers lived in a state of mental exile, constantly yearning for their homeland, as poignantly captured by V.S.

Naipaul.

This yearning sometimes translated into a desire to return, even if only to die in their janmabhumi (birthplace), as narrated in some accounts of returnees dressing in their best clothes, eagerly anticipating their homeland.

### Conclusion
Overall, while a notable portion of indentured laborers initially intended to return to India—and some did—various economic, social, and psychological factors, compounded by colonial policies, led the majority to remain in their colonial homes.

Their struggle and legacy form an essential narrative in understanding the broader history of Indian indenture and diaspora.
