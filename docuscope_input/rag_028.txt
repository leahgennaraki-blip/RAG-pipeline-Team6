- Indentured laborers were predominantly sourced from underprivileged backgrounds in India and China, driven by economic necessity.

They were assured of certain conditions, such as housing and food, but in practice, these promises were often unmet, leading to widespread exploitation.

**Commonalities and Differences**:
- Both coolies and indentured laborers were primarily Asian workers engaged in colonial and plantation economies across the globe, particularly after the abolition of slavery.

This confluence often led the terms to be used interchangeably.

- The key difference lies in the legal framework and implication: "indentured labourer" is a specific legal designation implying a signed contract and a fixed period of service, while "coolie" is more of a racialized epithet without legal nuance, emphasizing the subservient and dehumanized status imposed on these workers by their colonial employers.

- Both groups faced racial discrimination and harsh living conditions.

They were often housed in "coolie lines," primitive lodgings starkly contrasting with accommodations provided to European workers or even better-treated local laborers.
