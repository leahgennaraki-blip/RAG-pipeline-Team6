b.

Cultural Memory and Traditions
Cultural memory, which encompasses religious practices, languages, and festivals, became key ways in which indentured laborers maintained their identity in Suriname.

Through shared practices like Hindu rituals, Muslim prayers, and festivals (e.g., Diwali, Id-ul-Fitr), they created a new community identity that was rooted in their Indian heritage but also adapted to the Surinamese context.

Language played a crucial role in memory retention, as many indentured workers spoke Bhojpuri, Hindi, or Tamil.

These languages not only represented a connection to their ancestral homeland but also acted as a vehicle for cultural transmission across generations.

Even though the language evolved over time due to creolization, the memory of language maintained a sense of cultural continuity.

Transmission of Memory and Identity to New Generations
For the descendants of indentured laborers, identity continues to be shaped by memory, but it is also reinterpreted and reconstructed in ways that may differ from the original experiences of their ancestors.
