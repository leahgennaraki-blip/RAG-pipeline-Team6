These festivals offer a space for both cultural expression and the reaffirmation of group identity, as they are marked by communal gatherings, prayers, and traditional practices that have been handed down through generations.

However, there is also a shift in how these memories are perceived.

For instance, younger generations may feel that these celebrations are more about community bonding than the actual religious or cultural significance.

This shift reflects a broader trend where memory becomes symbolic, serving as a marker of belonging rather than an exact replication of ancestral practices.

Memory and Identity in a Multicultural Society
In Suriname, the integration of multiple ethnicities, including Afro-Surinamese, Javanese, Chinese, and Dutch, leads to a multicultural society where identities are not static but are constantly being negotiated and redefined.

For the descendants of indentured laborers, their identity is also shaped by their interaction with Afro-Surinamese culture and the broader national identity of Suriname.
