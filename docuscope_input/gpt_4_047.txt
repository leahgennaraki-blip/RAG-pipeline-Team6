Lower caste individuals, including those from the Dalit communities or those considered "untouchables" in India, were often treated with disdain or marginalized within the Indian community in Suriname.

The social stratification was evident in how people from different castes interacted with each other, with higher caste individuals often distancing themselves from the lower castes.

3.

Caste-based Communities in Suriname
The Indian community in Suriname became increasingly organized around caste lines, especially in terms of marriage and social functions.

Marriage within one's caste was seen as a way to preserve social purity and maintain the traditional roles and status associated with that caste.

Caste-based associations began to form in Suriname as a way for people from similar caste backgrounds to support each other socially and economically.

These groups were not just informal social networks but also played important roles in organizing religious and cultural events, such as Hindu festivals or rituals.

4.

Caste and Religion
Religion, particularly Hinduism, played a significant role in reinforcing caste-based distinctions in Suriname.

Although Hinduism in Suriname adapted to a Caribbean context, caste still played an important role in the religious life of the Indo-Surinamese community.
