The difference between written history and collective remembering about indenture primarily involves the sources, perspective, and purpose of the narratives.

**Written History:**
1.

**Sources:** Written histories of indenture typically rely on formal documents such as government records, contracts, laws, regulations, plantation reports, and archived material produced by colonial authorities.

These sources are often housed in national archives and libraries and reflect the official perspective of the time.

2.

**Perspective:** Written history tends to present a top-down view, influenced by the priorities and biases of the colonial regime.

This perspective might emphasize regulations, administrative processes, and economic outcomes, often overlooking the personal experiences of indentured laborers.

The colonial accounts could reflect a paternalistic view, depicting the indenture system as a regulated labor scheme offering protection and care, as suggested by official reports describing medical care, wages, and supervision.
