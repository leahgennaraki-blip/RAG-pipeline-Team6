Priests (Pandits), who were often from higher castes, played a central role in organizing religious ceremonies.

This created a religious hierarchy that mirrored the caste hierarchy in India.

Those from lower castes had to depend on higher caste priests for conducting religious rituals.

The caste system's influence could also be seen in temple management and ritual practices, where higher caste individuals often had more influence and were able to make decisions regarding religious leadership.

5.

Caste and Economic Roles
After indenture ended, caste identity influenced economic opportunities within the Indo-Surinamese community.

Many higher caste individuals took roles in the business sector, becoming merchants, landowners, or priests, while lower caste individuals continued to be involved in more manual labor or agricultural work.

Caste-based occupations continued to persist in Suriname, with certain roles (such as barbers, blacksmiths, or cleaners) being associated with lower castes, even in the post-indenture period.

6.

Caste and Social Mobility
The rigid boundaries of the caste system were somewhat challenged in the context of Suriname’s plantation society, where laborers were not confined to the same traditional occupations they would have held in India.

Nevertheless, many of the caste-based distinctions continued to affect social mobility.
