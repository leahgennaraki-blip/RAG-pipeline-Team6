While the opportunity to save existed due to the provision of rations, accommodation, and basic medical care by employers, the actual conditions on the ground, punitive deductions, and the arduous nature of the work often thwarted the potential for significant savings.

Despite these challenges, some labourers were able to save and eventually prosper after fulfilling their contracts, as indicated by anecdotes of individuals who successfully established themselves economically after their indenture period ended.
