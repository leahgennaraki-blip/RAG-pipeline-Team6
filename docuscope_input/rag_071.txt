**Economic Incentive**: In addition to land, a premium was sometimes offered to those who renounced their return rights and settled on government farms.

For instance, a decree in 1895 allowed settlers to retain the right to return after settlement and offered them a financial premium.

Additionally, land use was rent-free for six years, providing migrant farmers with a financial cushion.

5.

**Impact on Immigration Policy**: The land grant system reflected the broader trend of moving from immediate labor needs to long-term settlement goals.

It partly addressed labor shortages by turning temporary laborers into permanent residents.

Over time, many indentured laborers and their descendants settled permanently in Suriname, contributing significantly to its demographic diversity.

In summary, land grants in Suriname were designed as an incentive for indentured laborers to transition into permanent settlers, thus aiding the colony's shift from plantation-based economy to one where smallholder agriculture played a central role.

While initially met with hesitance due to the loss of repatriation rights, these grants ultimately contributed to the ethnic and cultural diversity characteristic of modern Suriname.
