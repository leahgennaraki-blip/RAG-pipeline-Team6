Moreover, the novel depicts a physical and metaphorical journey back to India, emphasizing a continued connection to the roots of indentured laborers even as they adapt to their new environment.

This journey reflects the broader theme of diaspora and the need for cultural adaptation and transculturation, highlighted by the diverse exchanges and relationships formed between different ethnic groups in Trinidad.

Through a blend of historical fiction and personal storytelling, *Jahajin* effectively translates the past for contemporary readers, using the protagonist's quest and Deeda's stories to underscore the enduring influence of the Indian indentured experience in shaping identities and histories in the Caribbean.
