Although the system was legally distinct from slavery, with contracts specifying wages and conditions, indentured laborers often encountered severe restrictions and abuses.

This fueled discontent and led to various forms of resistance.

### Key Incidents of Resistance

1.

**Refusal to Work and Arson**:
Frictions were common, as evidenced by the frequent refusal to work and instances of arson.

Laborers, frustrated by heavy workloads and low wages, often revolted against plantation management.

The Colonial Report of 1875 noted a ""spirit of disorder,"" indicating ongoing unrest among laborers.

2.

**The 1884 Plantations Revolt**:
A notable instance of resistance occurred in 1884 at the plantations Zoelen and Zorg en Hoop.

Reduced wages and increased work pressures during a sugar crisis led to the escalation of tensions.

At Zoelen, the district commissioner sided with plantation directors, which incited an attack on him by the workers.

At Zorg en Hoop, laborers resisted demands for more productivity, leading to an uprising when a supervisor was attacked and military intervention resulted in the shooting of seven Hindustani laborers after workers retaliated with stone-throwing when faced with demands for the surrender of their leaders.
