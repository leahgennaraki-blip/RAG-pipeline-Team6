The child slept on a sack.

When I went to cut cane, I tied him to my back or left him with another woman.

If he cried too much, the sirdar would shout that we were lazy.

There was no rest because I was a mother.

If I missed work because the child was sick, they marked me absent.

If I was absent, there was no pay.

If there was no pay, there was no food.

When my milk dried from hunger, the baby cried all night.

Sometimes the other women shared a little rice water.

That is how we kept each other alive.

The overseer did not care if a child had fever.

The law did not care.

The contract only knew cane.

I was afraid to leave my daughter alone.

Men drank.

Fights happened.

Sometimes women disappeared from one room to another.

We warned each other quietly.

Still, I taught my child our prayers.

I whispered stories from home so she would know she was not born only for the fields.

I washed her before dawn and told her she must live longer than this suffering.

When she learned to walk, she walked in mud and ash.
