White women involved with Black men could face corporal punishment and social ostracism, and Black men involved faced the death penalty.

The intense societal repulsion towards these relationships underscores the racial hierarchies upheld by colonial authorities.

3.

**Coloured and Free People of Colour:**
The interaction between free people of color and slaves also varied.

Despite being of non-white descent themselves, free colored individuals often held a perceived social superiority over slaves and aligned themselves closer to the colonial framework, even occupying positions like managers and overseers.

This created complex social dynamics, as they were both victims and perpetuators of racial discrimination.

### Community Reception

- **White Community:**
The white colonists often tried to maintain racial hierarchies, seeing interracial contacts, particularly those infringing upon class and racial boundaries, as disruptive to the colonial order.

White societal attitudes ranged from tacit acceptance of white men's interracial relationships—often due to economic and social convenience—to outright hostility towards relationships where white women engaged with Black or colored men.
