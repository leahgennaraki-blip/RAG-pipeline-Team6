In the post-colonial context of Suriname, the politics of identity for diasporic communities, particularly those of indentured Indian descent (referred to as Hindustanis in Suriname), operates within a framework of multiculturalism and ethnic consociationalism.

Suriname's history of ethnic diversity, driven by the importation of labor from various global regions, has resulted in a complex interplay of identities in the nation-state.

For diasporic communities, identity politics involve balancing the retention of their unique cultural heritage with integration into the broader Surinamese society.

### Identity Politics for Diasporic Communities in Suriname

1.

**Ethnicity and Multiculturalism:**
Suriname is characterized by its plural society where multiple ethnic groups, such as Hindustanis, Creoles, Javanese, and Maroons, coexist.

Each group maintains distinct cultural identities.

Ethnic identities are celebrated and preserved through cultural traditions, religious practices, and social institutions.
