Testimonies by people who experienced indenture—recorded through oral histories, memoirs, petitions, court records, and later interviews—present a consistent picture of coercion, hardship, resilience, and resistance.

Although filtered through memory and, at times, colonial mediation, these first-person accounts are among the most important sources for understanding indenture from the perspective of those who lived it.

What follows is a synthesized account drawn from multiple regions, with representative voices and recurring themes.
