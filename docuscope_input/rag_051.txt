The Arya Samaj, with its emphasis on returning to the teachings of the Vedas and its rejection of certain entrenched Hindu practices such as polytheism and caste-based discrimination, challenged traditional caste identities and customs.

This movement encouraged Hindus in Suriname to refer to their practice as Sanatan Dharma, stressing the eternal nature of their faith rather than rigid caste structures.

4.

**Socio-Economic Mobility**: Over time, the opportunity for economic and social mobility within Suriname also reduced the importance of caste.

As the descendants of these early Indian migrants integrated more with other ethnic groups and engaged in different occupations, the rigid social structure dictated by caste became less relevant.

5.

**Continued Influence in Social Status**: Although the traditional caste system did not maintain its formal structure, remnants of its influence persisted in subtle social statuses and distinctions, particularly among the Brahmins who managed to retain a degree of prestige due to their roles in religious rituals and societal functions.
