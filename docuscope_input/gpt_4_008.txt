But when we came, there was only cane and cane and whip."

Recruiters ("arkatis") often lied to villagers about the nature of the work and the conditions abroad.

2.

Voyage Across the Kala Pani (Black Water)
The sea journey itself was traumatic:

Testimony (Mauritius-bound ship, 1860s):

"We were packed together like goats.

Many died on the ship.

The smell of sickness was in the air.

The priests warned us that crossing the ocean would make us untouchable.

Some refused food and died fasting."

High mortality rates, disease (cholera, dysentery), and depression were common during the long voyages.

3.

Work on the Plantations
Once at the plantations, the shock was immense:

Testimony (Fiji, 1910):

"The sardar (overseer) would beat us if we paused even for a moment.

If we spoke of illness, they said we were lazy.

The sun burned us.

Sometimes we were made to work even when rain flooded the fields."

Wages were withheld or underpaid, and medical care was poor.

Women often faced sexual exploitation both from white overseers and from Indian male laborers.
