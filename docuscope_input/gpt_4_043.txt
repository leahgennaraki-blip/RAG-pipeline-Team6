Religious festivals and rituals became moments of collective solidarity, offering a sense of cultural pride and resilience.

In addition, Bhojpuri songs, folk tales, and oral histories preserved the laborers' memories and struggles while subtly protesting the alienating and dehumanizing conditions of plantation life.

These cultural forms kept alive the identity of the indentured laborers and provided a space to express dissent.

6.

Legal Petitions and Complaints
Laborers also sometimes resorted to legal petitions and formal complaints to challenge their treatment.

Although these petitions were often dismissed, they provided a formal avenue for laborers to express their grievances.

Complaints could be related to the harsh work conditions, lack of food, physical punishment, or issues such as fraudulent contracts and unfair wage practices.

These petitions sometimes had the backing of advocacy groups or individuals sympathetic to the plight of the laborers, such as missionaries or Indian activists, who played a role in drawing attention to the injustices of the indenture system.

7.

The Role of Trade Unions and Activists
After the abolition of indenture in many regions, trade unions and activist organizations became more prominent in advocating for the rights of former indentured workers and their descendants.
