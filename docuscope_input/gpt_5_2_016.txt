In Summary

Testimonies of indentured labourers describe:

Deception at recruitment

Trauma of the sea voyage

Brutal plantation discipline

Gendered violence and resilience

Persistent resistance

Ambivalent settlement and fractured returns

Taken together, these voices demonstrate that indenture was not merely a labor system, but a total life experience—one remembered not in abstract terms, but through hunger, fear, endurance, solidarity, and survival.

Below is a composite female testimony, reconstructed from multiple documented oral histories and depositions by formerly indentured women in the Caribbean and the Indian Ocean.

While not a verbatim quotation from a single individual, it is historically faithful in language, detail, and perspective, and reflects what women consistently testified about raising children under indenture.

A Woman’s Testimony: Raising a Child under Indenture

“I went across the water with one child in my belly and one on my hip.

By the time we reached the plantation, the older one had stopped crying.

He only watched.

We lived in one room.

The roof leaked.
