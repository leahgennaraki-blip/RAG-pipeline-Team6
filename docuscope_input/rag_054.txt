Festivals such as Diwali, Holi, and Eid are celebrated, and music and dance have become vital expressions of Indo-Surinamese identity, helping to forge a sense of community among the descendants of indentured labourers.

2.

**Language and Religion**: The indentured Indians brought with them languages like Hindi and Urdu, and religions such as Hinduism and Islam.

Sarnami, a variant of Hindi with Bhojpuri influences, and Sranan Tongo, a Creole language, have become linguistic legacies, facilitating communication amidst Suriname's ethnic diversity.

Hindu and Islamic practices have persisted, with organizations like the Arya Samaj playing a significant role in religious life.

3.

**Role of Women**: The experiences of indentured women, who faced unique challenges due to gender roles in both Indian and colonial societies, have left a lasting legacy of resilience and adaptability.

Their contribution to cultural practices and community building is remembered and celebrated, adding to the rich tapestry of Surinamese society.
