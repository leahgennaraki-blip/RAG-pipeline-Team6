- Recruitment efforts were challenged by logistical issues and the Chinese government's restrictions on emigration, only lifting capital punishment for emigration in 1866.

This caused the overall number of Chinese laborers in the Caribbean, including Suriname, to remain small.

- Economic motivations for migration stemmed chiefly from the dire social conditions in China, such as famine and local conflicts.

2.

**Indian Laborers**:
- Indian laborers began arriving in 1873, following a formal recruitment process established by the 1870 Anglo-Dutch treaty.

They primarily came from the United Provinces (Uttar Pradesh), Bengal, and West Bihar.

- The recruitment of Indians was more structured, largely because the British government in India regulated it to avoid abuses that had been prevalent during the initial indenture periods elsewhere.

- They were promised the right to return to India at the end of their contracts, although two-thirds opted to stay in Suriname permanently.

**Chinese Laborers**:
