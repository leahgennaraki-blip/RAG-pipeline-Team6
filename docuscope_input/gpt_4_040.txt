Despite the significant power imbalances and systemic exploitation, these laborers found various ways to resist and protest against their conditions of indenture.

Their protests took many forms, ranging from subtle acts of defiance to more organized uprisings.

Here’s an overview of the forms of protest by indentured laborers on the plantations:

1.

Work Slowdowns (Go-slow)
One of the most common forms of protest by indentured laborers was the work slowdown, often referred to as "go-slow".

Instead of outright refusal to work, workers would deliberately slow down the pace of their labor, thus reducing the overall productivity of the plantation.

This form of protest allowed workers to challenge their conditions without openly defying the overseers or plantation managers.

It was an effective method of passive resistance as it impacted plantation production without direct confrontation.

2.

Faking Illness and Desertion
Faking illness was another form of passive resistance employed by indentured laborers.

Workers would pretend to be sick or injured to avoid work, thereby disrupting the labor process.

Some laborers would desert the plantation or try to escape, often seeking refuge in nearby forests or among local communities.
