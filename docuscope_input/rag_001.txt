Peggy Mohan's *Jahajin* is a novel that delves into the historical and cultural legacy of Indian indenture in Trinidad, weaving a narrative that bridges the past and the present.

The story centers around an unnamed narrator, who is a translator of Trinidadian Bhojpuri, a language brought to Trinidad by Indian indentured migrants from north-eastern India.

This linguistic and cultural exploration forms the backbone of the novel, as the narrator engages in a process of translation and interpretation, not only linguistically but also historically, to uncover the obscured and often marginalized stories of Indian women who journeyed to Trinidad as indentured laborers.

The narrator's journey takes on a dual purpose: to document and preserve the Bhojpuri stories as a testament to the experiences of Indian women during the indenture period, and to bring these narratives into contemporary consciousness, providing what the novel terms "counter-histories" in response to official accounts that have minimized or concealed these contributions, particularly from a patriarchal viewpoint.

This process also connects the narrator personally to her own family's past, as she begins to empathize with her indentured ancestors, notably her great-great-grandmother.
