3.

Limited Access to Justice for Laborers
If laborers tried to file complaints or seek legal redress, they would face severe retaliation from plantation owners.

For instance, they might lose their jobs or face violence.

Some laborers attempted to petition the colonial authorities, but these petitions were usually dismissed or ignored.

When laborers were able to reach Dutch courts, they often found themselves at a disadvantage because the legal framework was so heavily biased against them.

4.

Corruption and Legal Bias
The corruption within the judicial system meant that plantation owners often had access to the most powerful legal networks and were able to use legal loopholes to avoid prosecution for crimes committed against workers.

Crimes against Indian laborers were not taken seriously by the colonial authorities, who considered them a disposable labor force.
