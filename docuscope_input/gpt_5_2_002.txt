Oral History and Memory: Deeda’s stories are rich in lived experience, conveying not just historical events but the emotions, traditions, and everyday realities of jahajins.

Folk Narrative Within Narrative: Embedded within the real-world migration story is a dreamlike folk tale about Saranga and Sada Birij, a mythic romance that reflects themes of love, loss and transformation, and functions as a symbolic counterpoint to the migrant experience.

Cultural Identity and Language: As a trained linguist, the narrator is attuned to how language—especially Bhojpuri—evolves, flourishes and fades in diaspora.

The book traces how linguistic change parallels cultural and generational shifts among the Indo-Trinidadian community.

Women's Writing Assignments, 2013

Women’s Agency: While the broader history of indenture often foregrounds male experiences, Jahajin centers female migrants and highlights their resilience, leadership and decisive roles in building new lives abroad.

The title itself emphasizes the sisterhood and shared journeys of these women.

Significance:
Jahajin serves as both a narrative of indentured migration and a meditation on memory, language, gender and identity.
