Indian Laborers and European Overseers/Managers

Relationships between Indian laborers and European overseers or plantation owners were more complicated, particularly because of the power dynamics involved.

European overseers were in positions of authority over the laborers, and sexual relationships were often seen as a form of abuse or exploitation.

In some cases, Indian women were forced into relationships with European overseers, and some laborers even had children as a result of such relationships.

These unions were not always voluntary, and women who resisted were often punished or ostracized.

These relationships were typically looked down upon by both the Indian and European communities.

Indian communities, particularly those who followed Hinduism or Islam, considered these unions as morally wrong, especially given the caste system’s strict norms regarding marriage and relationships.

The European overseers, on the other hand, often saw themselves as socially superior and may have viewed their relationships with Indian women as a reflection of their dominance.

Children born from these unions were often seen as illegitimate, and their social status was ambiguous.
