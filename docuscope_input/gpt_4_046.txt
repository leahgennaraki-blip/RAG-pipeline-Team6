Caste Identity among Indian Indentured Laborers
When the indentured laborers arrived in Suriname, many of them brought with them traditional social structures rooted in the caste system.

In India, the caste system organized people into hierarchical groups based on occupation, ritual purity, and hereditary status.

Upon arrival in Suriname, although the caste system was not explicitly enforced by the Dutch colonial authorities, it continued to manifest in the daily lives of the laborers.

Caste-based identities were still important for many of the indentured workers, particularly in relation to marriage, social interactions, and community organization.

Social Hierarchy on the Plantations
Within the plantation communities, a hierarchical structure emerged that mirrored aspects of the traditional caste system from India.

While all indentured laborers were technically of the same social status as "coolies", the division between higher and lower castes still persisted within the community.

Laborers from higher castes (like Brahmins or Vaishyas) often maintained some semblance of their privileges and social roles.

They were sometimes respected by other workers and tended to hold positions that allowed them to exercise some degree of authority or respect within the labor camp.
