Domestic labor was unrecognized and undervalued, despite its necessity for familial stability.

Married women's experiences were compounded by instances of domestic violence and abandonment.

Many women faced physical and emotional abuse from their husbands, who might also force them into prostitution to supplement family income.

Yet, this adversity sometimes prompted women to leave abusive partners, reflecting a quest for autonomy and safer circumstances.

### Widowed Women

Widows were particularly vulnerable within both Indian and colonial societal frameworks.

In India, societal norms restricted their rights, and they often lived marginal lives.

In the colonies, they experienced similar vulnerabilities.

With the social safety net back home weakened, widowhood became a dire state due to its association with poverty and limited social support.

Widows who migrated often did so to escape the constraints placed on them at home, seeking new opportunities abroad.

Once in the colonies, some widows remarried or formed new partnerships for financial support, exploiting the freedom from traditional constraints, though this could also lead to social censure.

Widows, like single women, occasionally leveraged their scarcity and the consequent demand to secure better positions or relationships.
