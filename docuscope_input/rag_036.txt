### Nature of the Judicial System:

1.

**Bias and Partiality**:
- The judiciary was heavily biased in favor of plantation owners.

Magistrates and other judicial officers were often either plantation owners themselves or closely aligned with them, leading to partial judgments.

2.

**Prosecution and Conviction Rates**:
- Prosecution rates of indentured laborers were significantly high; about one-fifth to one-third of them were prosecuted annually.

In contrast, successful convictions against managers and overseers were rare.

3.

**Lax Enforcement against Owners**:
- Despite legal provisions, enforcement against planters for violations such as physical brutality was ineffective.

Convictions were rare and penalties, if given, were often trivial, typically fines rather than imprisonment.

4.

**Limited Avenues for Laborers**:
- Laborers faced significant barriers in laying complaints due to their lack of knowledge of the law, inexperience, and the risk of retaliation.

Complaints were seldom pursued by the Immigration Department or resulted in significant relief.

5.

**Use of Penal Sanctions**:
- The judicial system supported penal sanctions that allowed extension of labor contracts for absences and minor infractions, thus legally trapping laborers in a cycle of perpetual servitude.
