The unmet need for women on plantations allowed some to negotiate new family formations, but they still faced societal stigma primarily due to their gender and marital status.

### General Observations

Across the board, women’s experiences were marred by the colonial system's failures to ensure fairness and protection.

Both the socioeconomic and legal structures were typically biased against women, locking them into roles and identities imposed by patriarchal and colonial ideologies.

However, women found ways to assert their agency despite these oppressive circumstances, whether through economic activities, navigating personal relationships, or leveraging their labor value to negotiate better terms for themselves and their families.

Ultimately, the treatment of women – single, married, and widowed – in colonial settings was characterized by a complex interplay of oppression and resistance, shaped by the broader forces of gender, race, and class within which they operated.
