**Decolonization Trauma:**
- The term 'decolonization trauma' has been used to describe Dutch reluctance to relinquish control over its colonies, especially Indonesia.

This period was marked by significant violence during the so-called 'police actions,' which many international observers labeled as colonial warfare.

The Dutch struggled to come to terms with this violent past, with debates persisting into the late 20th century over how to address historical wrongs.

3.

**Ethical Policy:**
- The ethical policy movement, which gained traction in the early 20th century, was partly a response to critiques like those from Van Deventer, who highlighted "diminished prosperity" among colonized populations due to exploitative practices.

This acknowledgment led to some investments in improving conditions, although it fell short of a full embrace of the violence and exploitation within the system.

### Attempts at Reparations:

1.

**Monuments and Solemn Declarations:**
- Since the late 1990s, the Dutch government has supported initiatives to commemorate the Atlantic slave trade and slavery.

Monuments in Amsterdam and initiatives by NiNsee reflect a public acknowledgment of the historical atrocities tied to slavery.

Public figures, including members of the royal family, have expressed remorse, although official apologies have been inconsistent.
