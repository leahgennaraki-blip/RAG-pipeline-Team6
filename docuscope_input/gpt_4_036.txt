Bhojpuri-speaking laborers brought with them a rich tradition of oral storytelling, folk songs, and ritual chants.

In Suriname, these traditional songs were adapted to new circumstances, blending with local cultural practices and rhythms.

Bhojpuri songs in Suriname began to reflect the experiences of the laborers in the colonial environment, expressing themes such as hardship, nostalgia for India, love, and struggle for freedom.

Chutney music, which emerged from these Bhojpuri traditions, is a fusion of Indian and Caribbean musical styles, featuring Bhojpuri lyrics paired with the rhythms of Calypso and Soca music.

This music became a vital means of cultural expression for the Indian diaspora, combining the laborers’ Indian roots with the Caribbean context they found themselves in.

Religious and Ritual Practices
The indentured laborers also carried their religious practices with them, primarily Hinduism and Islam.

The use of Bhojpuri in religious rituals allowed for the adaptation of traditional Indian prayers and Hindu hymns into the local context, creating a fusion of cultural practices.

Over time, Bhojpuri became the language of Hindu festivals and ceremonies in Suriname, even though Dutch and Javanese were also present in the community.
