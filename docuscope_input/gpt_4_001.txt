Peggy Mohan's Jahajin is a debut novel that intertwines history, folklore, and personal narratives to explore the experiences of Indian indentured laborers in Trinidad.

The story is framed through the perspective of a young linguist researching the Bhojpuri language in Trinidad, who encounters Deeda, a 110-year-old woman who made the journey from India to Trinidad as a child.

Deeda shares her life story, recounting her departure from Basti, India, with her son, and the arduous voyage across the "kala pani" (black water) to "Chini-dad" (the land of sugar), where she worked on the Esperanza estate.

Interwoven with Deeda's narrative is the myth of Saranga, a tale about a female monkey torn between her monkey lover and a human prince.

This allegorical story reflects themes of transformation, choice, and identity, paralleling the real-life experiences of the indentured laborers.
