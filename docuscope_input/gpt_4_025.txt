Forced Labor Beyond Contract Terms
Plantation owners often exploited workers by forcing them to work beyond the terms of their indenture contracts.

Laborers were made to work in harsh conditions, including longer hours and on lesser wages than agreed upon.

When workers reached the end of their contracts, they were frequently prevented from leaving.

Debt bondage was a common tactic used to trap laborers: workers who wanted to leave would be told that they owed money for expenses or that their contract had been renewed without their consent.

Denial of Basic Needs
Adequate food and medical care were frequently denied.

Laborers were given poor quality rations, and if they fell ill, overseers often ignored their needs, leading to widespread disease and death.

Inhumane living conditions in barracks meant that laborers often lived in overcrowded, unsanitary environments, leading to outbreaks of diseases like malaria, dysentery, and tuberculosis.

5.

Kidnapping and Forced Migration
Some workers were kidnapped or tricked into becoming indentured laborers.

Agents would often deceive people in India by offering them promises of better conditions in the colonies, only to find themselves locked into indentured contracts once they arrived in Suriname.
