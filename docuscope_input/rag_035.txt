Such abuses included flogging and other forms of corporal punishment.

Women faced additional threats of or actual sexual abuse, particularly from young, unmarried white overseers and Indian sirdars (foremen).

2.

**Exploitation and Deception**:
- Planters routinely misrepresented the conditions of work and the wages that indentured laborers would receive.

Wages were frequently delayed, reduced, or deducted arbitrarily for minor infractions that were not legally sanctioned.

3.

**Intimidation and Coercion**:
- Plantation officials intimidated laborers into re-indenture by threatening them with prison for minor violations of contract or work discipline.

Resistance or protest was often met with overwhelming coercion, including violence and unjust legal action.

4.

**Disregard for Workers’ Welfare**:
- Laborers were subject to excessively demanding tasks, often set at the pace of the most productive workers, which many could not meet, leading to further legal action against them.

Basic needs, such as adequate rest, health care, and sanitation, were neglected.

5.

**Criminalization of Minor and Fabricated Offenses**:
- Laborers were charged with a range of minor offenses such as 'habitual idling' or 'absenteeism', and were frequently penalized for these.

The ordinance allowed conviction for ludicrous charges such as defecating within certain areas, leading to significant fines and jail time.
