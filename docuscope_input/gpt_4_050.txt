Despite these tensions, the Dutch colonial authorities did not actively enforce caste distinctions.

However, the division between castes remained an underlying social reality that influenced many aspects of Indo-Surinamese life, even as the indentured system came to an end.

9.

Impact of Other Communities on Caste Practices
The interactions between the Indian indentured laborers and other ethnic groups, such as the Africans, Javanese, and Chinese in Suriname, also had an influence on how caste was expressed.

Some caste-based practices were eroded or modified through these interactions, while others persisted as part of the Indo-Surinamese community’s internal social fabric.

Conclusion
While the caste system did not operate in Suriname in the same way it did in India, it reinvented itself in a form adapted to the colonial and plantation environment.

The caste system found expression in social hierarchy, religious practices, economic roles, and marriage within the Indo-Surinamese community.

Over time, though, caste identities have become less rigid, especially in more recent years, as social dynamics have changed and as Indo-Surinamese society has become more integrated into the larger Surinamese national identity.

However, the legacy of caste continues to shape aspects of cultural identity and social relations within the Indo-Surinamese population.
