It’s not just about what is recorded, but about how groups of people – in this case, the descendants of indentured laborers – interpret and pass on their own experiences and histories.

Characteristics:
Subjective and Personal: Collective remembering is deeply shaped by personal experiences, emotions, and community narratives.

It’s about how individuals and groups remember the past in ways that are often informal, dynamic, and fluid.

Community-Centric: It often involves the oral transmission of stories, folklore, and traditions that are passed down from one generation to the next.

These memories are shaped by the community’s values, needs, and understandings of their history.

Bottom-up: This form of memory often emerges from the grassroots, with individuals or groups who experienced indenture (or whose ancestors did) telling their own stories.

As a result, it might emphasize personal survival stories, emotional resilience, and cultural preservation that aren’t necessarily reflected in the official written history.

Cultural and Emotional Focus: Collective remembering often highlights shared cultural practices, such as the preservation of language (e.g., Hindi, Bhojpuri), religion (Hinduism, Islam), and customs (like festivals and rituals).
