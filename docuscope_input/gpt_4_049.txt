Despite the economic opportunities that came with post-indentured life, the ability to rise through the ranks of Suriname’s social structure was still often influenced by one’s caste background.

Higher caste people were generally more likely to integrate into elite circles, while lower caste people were often stuck in more menial positions.

7.

Caste and Marriage
Arranged marriages within the same caste continued to be a prominent practice among the Indian laborers and their descendants in Suriname.

The caste system continued to regulate who could marry whom, despite the fact that indentured laborers had been drawn from a variety of regions across India.

There were occasional inter-caste marriages, but these were often discouraged or looked down upon by community leaders from higher castes.

Marriages between high-caste and low-caste individuals were a source of social tension, as lower caste people were often seen as "impure" by the higher caste community.

8.

Emerging Caste-based Tensions
Over time, as the Indo-Surinamese community became more established and interconnected, tensions between castes within the community began to emerge more visibly.

These tensions were fueled by economic disparities, especially as some castes gained economic advantages over others.
