Indian Laborers and Indigenous or African Communities

Though not as common as relationships between Indian laborers and Chinese or European individuals, there were instances where Indian indentured laborers formed relationships with African-descended workers (enslaved or freed) or indigenous peoples on the plantations.

These relationships were often more acceptable within the African or indigenous communities, though the social and racial hierarchy of the time still created boundaries.

Over time, some Indian-Afro-Surinamese and Indian-Indigenous communities were formed, particularly in places like Suriname and Trinidad, where such mixed communities eventually became part of the cultural fabric.

Reception of Inter-Racial Relationships
1.

Within the Indian Community
Indian laborers who formed relationships outside of their community, particularly with European overseers or Chinese laborers, often faced social ostracism.

The caste system was extremely important in Indian society, and inter-caste relationships or marriages were generally forbidden.

The colonial context complicated this, as Indian workers had little control over their relationships and were often forced into them.

Indian women who were involved in such relationships were particularly vulnerable, as they could be seen as dishonored or tainted, especially if they had children from these unions.
