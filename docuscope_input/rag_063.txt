In summary, while written history on indenture often presents a structured, institutional view that could be limited by official narratives, collective remembering offers a more nuanced, human-centered understanding, emphasizing the complexity, continuity, and richness of the indentured labor experience as lived and retold within communities.

This duality allows for a more comprehensive appreciation of indenture's legacy, as both a historical and ongoing socioeconomic and cultural phenomenon.
