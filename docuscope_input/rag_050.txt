There were several reasons for this, rooted in the social, economic, and cultural changes that the Indian indentured laborers experienced upon their migration to Suriname.

1.

**Shared Labor and Conditions**: In Suriname, the caste system’s influence waned primarily due to the shared labor conditions on the plantations.

Regardless of their caste backgrounds, all indentured laborers were subjected to the same work and living conditions.

This encouraged a degree of equality and solidarity among the laborers, significantly weakening the traditional caste hierarchies that they might have adhered to in India.

2.

**Shortage of Women and Mixed Marriages**: The shortage of women among the early Indian migrants and the prolonged periods living together during transit and in labor depots encouraged the formation of mixed marriages.

This further diluted strict adherence to caste norms, especially the tradition of marrying within one’s own caste.

These circumstances promoted greater inter-caste interaction and integration.

3.

**The Influence of Arya Samaj**: The arrival of the Arya Samaj movement in Suriname during the 1920s also played a role in transforming traditional caste practices.
