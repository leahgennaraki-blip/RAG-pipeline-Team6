Rituals like Diwali, Holi, and Shivaratri continued to be celebrated, and the Bhojpuri language found a new expression in religious chants, storytelling, and songs that resonated deeply with the indentured laborers' sense of identity.

4.

The Emergence of New Bhojpuri Literature
The laborers' experiences also gave rise to new forms of literature in Bhojpuri, including oral literature that expressed their hardships and dreams.

The language evolved to accommodate the new cultural realities of the plantation workers in Suriname.

In the post-indenture period, poets and storytellers from the Indo-Surinamese community began to craft written works that addressed themes of migration, identity, and the complex hybridity of their existence.

These new forms of expression were often bilingual, blending Bhojpuri with Dutch or local creoles.

The literary expressions reflected the diasporic experience and helped to preserve the language for future generations.

5.

The Role of Bhojpuri in Surinamese Identity and Nationalism
After indenture ended, many descendants of indentured laborers continued to speak Bhojpuri, though it began to diminish in favor of Dutch and Sranan Tongo (the local creole).

However, Bhojpuri still holds a strong cultural identity among the Indo-Surinamese people.
