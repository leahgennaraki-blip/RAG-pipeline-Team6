Authoritative: Written history is often produced by historians, scholars, or authorities such as governments, universities, or institutions, and is usually considered the authoritative version of events.

Top-down: The historical accounts written in books or articles are frequently shaped by the perspective of those in positions of power.

The voices of marginalized groups, such as the indentured laborers, might be omitted or underrepresented, or their experiences might be filtered through the lens of colonial powers.

Focused on Political and Economic Structures: Written history tends to focus on the political, economic, and institutional aspects of indenture.

It might emphasize topics like the Dutch colonial system, the recruitment of indentured laborers, the conditions on plantations, and the end of indenture.

Example:
An academic book or a government report about the indentured labor system in Suriname might outline facts about the number of laborers brought to the country, their working conditions, and legal frameworks, but it could lack a personal, emotional, or subjective perspective on what it was like to experience indenture firsthand.
