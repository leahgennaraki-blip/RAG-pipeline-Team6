Here's how Bhojpuri found new cultural expression through indentured labor in Suriname:

1.

Retention and Evolution of Bhojpuri Among Indentured Laborers
Bhojpuri was initially brought to Suriname by Indian indentured laborers, most of whom came from the Bihar and Uttar Pradesh regions of India, where Bhojpuri was widely spoken.

For many of these laborers, the language became a crucial part of their identity and culture, helping them to maintain a sense of continuity with their homeland.

However, after years of isolation from India, the language began to evolve.

Contact with other ethnic groups—especially African, Dutch, and Javanese communities—caused Bhojpuri to blend with other languages, resulting in the development of a unique Surinamese Bhojpuri dialect.

Over time, this dialect incorporated influences from the local creole languages, as well as words from Dutch, African languages, and even Javanese, reflecting the multicultural environment of the plantation.
