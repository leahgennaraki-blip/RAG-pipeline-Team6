Context of "Lang" in Suriname:
Completion of the Initial Indenture Contract:

Indentured workers in Suriname typically signed a contract that lasted for five years, during which they were supposed to work on the plantations in exchange for food, shelter, and a modest wage.

At the end of this period, the workers were often given the opportunity to return to India or to remain in Suriname and work for pay.

Granting of "Lang":

However, many indentured workers were not allowed to leave once their contract was completed.

The system of "lang" meant that plantation owners could demand additional labor from the workers, extending their indenture for another period of time (sometimes several months to a year or more) under coercive conditions.

This was often done without adequate compensation, beyond what had already been agreed upon in the contract.

Reasons for "Lang" Extension: The extension of labor through "lang" was justified by various reasons, such as debts incurred by the laborers during their time on the plantations, the uncertainty regarding their freedom to leave the plantation, or the unwillingness of workers to leave without proper means of survival.
