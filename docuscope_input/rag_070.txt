However, as economic strategies evolved and the fashion of equating progress with population growth gained popularity, the government began promoting the idea of populating Suriname with smallholders.

This led to policies that facilitated land acquisition for migrants, intending for them to also serve as auxiliary labor for the plantations.

2.

**Land Acquisition Conditions**: Asian immigrants who completed their labor contracts and possessed a certificate of good behavior could obtain uncultivated plots of land under favorable conditions.

These plots were granted free of charge for a period of six years.

However, the responsibility lay with the settlers to cultivate the land and maintain crucial infrastructure like dikes and sluices, which were essential due to the low-lying geography of the Guianas.

3.

**Challenges and Incentives**: There was a significant catch to accepting the land offer: migrants who opted for land grants forfeited their entitlement to a free return passage to their homelands, which was originally one of the incentives provided under their contracts.

This deterred many laborers from taking up the offer, given the potential risks and economic pressures back home that had originally driven them to seek work overseas.
