In the mid-20th century, as Suriname became more aware of its multicultural and multilingual heritage, Bhojpuri underwent a resurgence as a symbol of cultural pride.

The language became a marker of Indo-Surinamese identity, particularly in the context of nationalism and the fight for cultural recognition within Surinamese society.

In the 1980s and beyond, there was a revival movement of Bhojpuri, with new generations striving to preserve and celebrate the language through songs, stories, and theatre.

Even today, efforts continue in preserving and documenting Bhojpuri as an important cultural and linguistic heritage.

Bhojpuri as a Medium of Communication Across Communities
Bhojpuri, in its modified Surinamese form, served as a bridge for communication among the Indo-Surinamese, Chinese, African, and Javanese laborers, facilitating interaction between communities that otherwise spoke different languages.

Though there were tensions between different ethnic groups, Bhojpuri, particularly in the form of oral traditions, helped create a shared space for expression and solidarity in a racially segregated society.

For instance, the Chutney genre, sung in Bhojpuri, became popular not just among the Indian diaspora but also among other ethnic groups in Suriname, contributing to a shared cultural space.
