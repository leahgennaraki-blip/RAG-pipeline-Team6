Instead, nationalist discourse often defaults to a Creole-based understanding, which is perceived as exclusionary by non-Creole groups like the Hindustanis.

Thus, for many Hindustanis, national identity is dual-faceted, involving both a sense of belonging to the Surinamese state and an adherence to ethnic traditions.

5.

**Challenges of Representation and Recognition:**
The Hindustani community has historically been underrepresented in political leadership, often playing "second fiddle" to the Creole group.

Despite this, Hindustanis have developed strong communal and cultural networks within Suriname, maintaining ties with other Hindustani diasporic communities, and supporting education and economic advancement for future generations.

6.

**Resistance to Nationalism:**
Identity politics in Suriname often involve a resistance to the Creole-dominated nationalism.

The rise of apanjaht discourses, focused on power-sharing and ethnic group recognition, counteracts pressures to homogenize into a singular national identity.

This resistance is not oriented towards separatism but rather towards ensuring that ethnic identities are acknowledged and respected within the political system.

### Affinity of Indentured Indians

Considering the above dynamics, the indentured Indians in Suriname find affinity in multiple layers of identity.

They retain a strong connection to their Indian roots, cultural practices, and familial networks.
