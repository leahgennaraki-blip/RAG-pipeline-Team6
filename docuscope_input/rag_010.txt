**Deductions and Penalties:** Employers frequently implemented deductions from wages for absences, non-completion of an assigned task, or other infractions.

Some planters practiced "double cut," docking two days' wages for a single day's absence.

Such deductions often severely impacted the actual earnings of the labourers.

3.

**Payment Delays and Variability:** Payments were frequently delayed, with wages often only settled at the end of the month rather than weekly, increasing the disadvantage to the labourers.

Variations in payment schedules made it difficult for labourers to contest disputes over wages and compounded their financial struggles.

4.

**Overwork and Illness:** Many labourers suffered from overwork, malnutrition, and inadequate health care, which further impacted their ability to earn the contracted wages consistently.

Low wages also contributed to poor diets, leading to more sickness and absenteeism, thereby perpetuating a cycle of low earnings.

5.

**Task-based and Time-based Work Options:** Although labourers were given the choice between timework (i.e., working nine hours daily) and taskwork, the latter determined by the amount of work deemed reasonable for an able-bodied adult to complete in six hours, the physical toll and unrealistic expectations often rendered these options inequitable.
