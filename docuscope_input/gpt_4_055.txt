Public memorials and commemoration events are held to honor the laborers who endured such hardships, and there are also museums and heritage sites dedicated to the history of indenture in Suriname.

These events allow for the reflection on the experiences of the indentured laborers and offer an opportunity for healing and solidarity within the community.

The Indo-Surinamese diaspora also maintains close ties with India, and many view their legacy as a bridge between Indian culture and Surinamese society.

The Indian community continues to celebrate its shared ancestry, which connects it not just to Suriname but also to India and the wider global Indian diaspora.

7.

The Emotional Toll and the Continuing Struggle for Justice
For many in the community, the indenture system is still a source of emotional pain and historical trauma.

The experiences of exploitation, displacement, and loss are acknowledged, and there are ongoing efforts to bring attention to these injustices.

Generations of Surinamese people of Indian descent have worked to ensure that the historical significance of indenture is not forgotten and that the suffering of their ancestors is recognized and respected.
