These abuses occurred within a highly exploitative system that left indentured workers extremely vulnerable, both socially and legally.

Below is an exploration of the kinds of crimes committed and the judicial system that allowed these abuses to persist.

Crimes Committed by Plantation Owners and Overseers
1.

Physical Abuse and Violence
Corporal punishment was commonly used to enforce discipline.

Beatings with whips or sticks were routine for minor infractions like late arrival at work, poor performance, or disobedience.

Laborers were often punished for minor offenses, such as speaking in their native language instead of English or Dutch (which was enforced as the plantation language).

Murder was not uncommon, especially in cases where laborers were suspected of disobedience or escaping.

In some instances, overseers or plantation managers would kill or severely injure workers, but this rarely led to prosecution.

2.

Sexual Exploitation
Female laborers, particularly single women or widows, were frequently victims of sexual abuse by plantation owners and overseers.

Rape and harassment were common, and many women were forced into sexual relationships with their supervisors or plantation managers.

If women resisted, they could face punishment, including being forced to work longer hours or being denied food.
