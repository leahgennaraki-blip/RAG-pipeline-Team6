**Land Ownership and Agriculture**: With the offer of land after completing their contracts, many indentured labourers became small farmers, helping to transform Suriname's agricultural landscape.

This shift from plantation labour to smallholder farming marked a significant socio-economic transition, supporting the community's economic self-sufficiency.

**Struggle for Rights and Representation**: Post-World War II, the Indian community in Suriname actively sought political integration and equal representation, striving to overcome the legacy of socio-economic marginalization perpetuated by the indenture system.

They have since played important roles in Surinamese politics and society.

### Reflection and Resilience

1.

**Historical Reflection**: There's a nuanced view of indenture within the Surinamese Indian community.

While the system brought new opportunities and economic benefits for some, it also involved hardships, stringent conditions, and cultural dislocation.

Debates continue between perspectives viewing it as a form of "new slavery" versus those emphasizing the agency and opportunity it offered to escape adverse conditions in India.

2.

**Commemoration and Research**: The history of indenture is commemorated through cultural events and academic research that highlight the experiences and contributions of indentured labourers and their descendants.
