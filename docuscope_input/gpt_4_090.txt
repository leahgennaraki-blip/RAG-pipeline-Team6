Historical Justice and Recognition
Many descendants of indentured laborers call for official recognition of the indenture system and the abuses endured by their ancestors.

They argue that the system was exploitative and brutal, and that its legacy continues to affect their economic and social positions.

Personal Accounts of Trauma and Suffering
Individually, many members of the community express a deep emotional and psychological connection to the struggles of their ancestors.

For example, individuals in the community may speak about family stories handed down across generations that detail the physical violence, forced labor, and dehumanization that their ancestors suffered.

These personal accounts often evoke a sense of betrayal and a desire for acknowledgment of the suffering, which many feel has been marginalized in historical accounts.

One individual, Sushila, a second-generation Indo-Surinamese woman, recounts how her grandmother, who was an indentured laborer, would weep while telling stories of being forced to work under harsh conditions with very little rest.

"We were treated like cattle," she recalls her grandmother saying.

For Sushila, these memories are a constant reminder of the injustice her family endured.
