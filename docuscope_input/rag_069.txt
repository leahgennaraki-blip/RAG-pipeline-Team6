In Suriname, the policy surrounding land grants to indentured laborers was introduced as part of efforts to transition from a temporary labor system to one encouraging permanent settlement, particularly as a response to the challenges faced by the colony's economy after the abolition of slavery in 1863.

As the colonial government shifted its focus from saving large plantations to promoting permanent settlements and smallholder agriculture in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, land grants became a strategic tool to encourage indentured laborers, particularly those from Asia, to stay in Suriname after completing their contracts.
