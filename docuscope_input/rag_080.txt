**Indentured Labor as 'New Slavery':** The indenture system is compared to slavery, with its elements of coercion, exploitation, and severe restrictions on personal freedoms.

This comparison forms the basis of the call for reparations, as it recognizes the historical wrongs akin to slavery that need to be addressed.

This view argues that, much like the descendants of African slaves, the descendants of indentured laborers are owed reparations for the systemic exploitation and degradation faced by their ancestors.

2.

**Testimonios and Oral Narratives:** Testimonios, such as those from laborers like Sanadhya and individuals involved in testifying about their experiences, provide a poignant picture of indenture life, documenting the abuses faced and forming a part of the collective memory demanding acknowledgment and reparations.

These narratives highlight personal grievances and the psychological scars left by indenture, making a compelling argument for reparations as a form of restorative justice.

3.

**Socio-Economic Impact and Cultural Displacement:** The fragmentation of traditional cultural practices, familial structures, and socio-economic instability, as documented in these narratives, underscores the long-term impact of indenture.

This cultural dislocation and socio-economic marginalization have fueled demands for reparations aimed at providing financial restitution and cultural rehabilitation for descendant communities.
