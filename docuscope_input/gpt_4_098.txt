This ethnic divide has had lasting implications for the way Indo-Surinamese people engage with Surinamese nationalism and their role in the nation-state.

b.

Indo-Surinamese Nationalism
In response to the Afro-Surinamese nationalism, Indo-Surinamese communities have often mobilized around a cultural nationalism that emphasizes their Indian heritage.

This nationalism is expressed through:

Cultural festivals (such as Diwali and Holi)

Religious practices (the celebration of Hinduism and Islam)

Language preservation efforts (in languages such as Bhojpuri and Hindi)

This form of cultural nationalism is sometimes seen as an attempt to assert their place in Suriname’s national identity, particularly in the face of historical marginalization.

c.

Indo-Surinamese Political Engagement
In politics, Indo-Surinamese people have made efforts to preserve and protect their interests.

Over time, political parties and movements have emerged that represent the community’s social, economic, and cultural concerns.

This includes calls for greater representation in government and the preservation of their cultural identity.

There are political leaders in Suriname who draw on Indo-Surinamese nationalism, and they frequently emphasize the importance of recognizing the contribution of Indian indentured laborers to Suriname’s development.
