Escaping was risky, as it could result in severe punishment if caught, but it was a form of protest against the brutal conditions of indenture.

Desertions and runaways were not uncommon, and the colonial authorities often took harsh measures to recapture those who fled.

3.

Strikes and Collective Action
Strikes were one of the more open forms of resistance, where laborers would collectively refuse to work to demand better wages, working conditions, or other benefits.

These strikes were often sparked by grievances over low wages, excessive hours, or poor living conditions.

In some cases, strikes were organized by Indian workers, but they were usually isolated incidents due to the fear of retaliation.

However, when these strikes did occur, they could create significant disruption to plantation operations.

For example, in Trinidad and Guyana, workers who had been subjected to poor treatment on the plantations occasionally protested by staging collective walkouts.

4.

Rebellions and Uprisings
The Indian indentured laborers in Suriname, along with workers in other colonies, were not passive in the face of their oppression.

In some cases, these tensions exploded into armed uprisings.

These rebellions were violent protests in response to brutal conditions, unjust treatment, and racist colonial policies.
