Cultural Reparations
In addition to financial reparations, many in the community also demand cultural reparations to help preserve and celebrate the heritage of Indian indentured laborers in Suriname.

The laborers brought with them a rich cultural legacy—language, religion, customs—that has had a lasting influence on Suriname’s cultural landscape.

However, this heritage is often seen as undervalued or even erased in national narratives.

Preservation of Language and Religion
A growing number of community members believe that cultural preservation is a form of reparations.

Bhojpuri, Hindi, and other Indian languages have become less common among younger generations, as the focus in Suriname has shifted to Dutch or Sranan Tongo.
