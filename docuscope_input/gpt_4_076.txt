Conditions of "Lang":

During the period of "lang," workers were often subjected to poor working conditions, and their ability to negotiate the terms of their labor was extremely limited.

They had already signed their initial contracts under duress and had little leverage to refuse the extension.

These extensions were used as a way to ensure that the plantation owners had a constant labor supply, and the workers were sometimes trapped in a cycle of labor exploitation, making it harder for them to accumulate the means to leave the plantation.

Legal Framework and Exploitation:

The legal system in Suriname supported this extension of indenture under the guise of debt or legal obligations, often siding with plantation owners.

Plantation owners used the "lang" extension as a way to control workers long after their contracts had technically expired, reinforcing the systematic exploitation of labor.

Impact on Workers:

The practice of "lang" effectively trapped many indentured laborers in a cycle of forced labor that extended their time on the plantations far beyond what was originally agreed upon in their contracts.
