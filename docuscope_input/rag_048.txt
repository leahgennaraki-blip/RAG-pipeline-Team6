**Cultural and Symbolic Acts**: Resistance was also expressed through cultural defiance, such as the recitation of religious texts or the destruction of symbolic objects like burning effigies, which represented the indenture system itself.

This allowed laborers to maintain a sense of identity and morality despite the oppressive conditions.

7.

**Exodus**: In some regions, mass departures from plantations, known as exodus, acted as a potent form of protest.

Workers would collectively abandon their posts, symbolizing a mass rejection of the imposed living and working conditions.

8.

**Suicidal Acts**: Unfortunately, in some extreme cases, suicide was a form of protest against the helplessness felt under the system, though reports of this were often sidelined or misunderstood by colonial authorities.

These forms of protest demonstrate not only the ingenuity and resilience of the indentured laborers but also the oppressive circumstances that necessitated such acts.

The systemic barriers to organized resistance—such as geographical dispersion, ethnic and caste differences, and the overwhelming power of colonial legal and social structures—made these acts of dissent particularly significant.

Despite not always achieving immediate success, they nonetheless highlighted the injustices inherent in the labor system and contributed to the gradual push toward reform and eventual abolition of indenture.
