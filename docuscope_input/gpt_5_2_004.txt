Resistance by indentured labourers in Suriname was sustained, multifaceted, and continuous throughout the period of indenture (1873–1916).

Although colonial archives often frame indentured workers as passive or compliant, historical evidence shows that Indian, Javanese, and other labourers actively resisted exploitation through everyday acts, collective action, and open rebellion.

Context: Indenture in Suriname

After the abolition of slavery in the Dutch Caribbean (1863, with enforced “apprenticeship” until 1873), plantation owners in Suriname imported indentured labourers, primarily from British India and Java.

Indian indentured workers—known locally as Hindostanis—arrived under five-year contracts to work mainly on sugar, coffee, and cocoa plantations.
