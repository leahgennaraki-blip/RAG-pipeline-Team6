Family and community reputation were highly valued, so any transgression was often met with harsh judgment.

In some cases, however, Indian men who formed relationships with Chinese women or African women could be more accepted, depending on the circumstances and the status of the women involved.

2.

Within the Chinese Community
Chinese laborers were often viewed as more privileged than their Indian counterparts, especially because they were sometimes placed in supervisory roles or seen as skilled laborers.

Relationships between Chinese men and Indian women were sometimes tolerated, but not always welcomed by Chinese families.

Chinese communities in Suriname, for example, were small compared to Indian communities, and marriage outside the group was discouraged but not entirely prohibited.

Chinese men who married Indian women, or vice versa, might have been looked down upon, as these unions were seen as a threat to cultural identity.

3.

Within the European Community
European overseers and plantation managers who engaged in relationships with Indian laborers were typically seen as exploitative, and their actions could be condoned as long as it benefited the plantation economy.

However, such relationships were not usually publicly acknowledged, especially if children were involved.
