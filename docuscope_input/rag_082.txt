Their contemporary struggles for equal rights and socio-economic development in post-colonial societies form part of the broader reparations discourse, demanding historical accountability and tangible rectification measures.

In sum, the demand for reparations by the indentured community is deeply intertwined with historical narratives of exploitation, systemic injustice, and the ongoing socio-economic struggles faced by descendants.

These narratives collectively argue for reparations as a necessary means of addressing historical wrongs and ensuring equity and acknowledgement for the enduring legacy of indenture.
